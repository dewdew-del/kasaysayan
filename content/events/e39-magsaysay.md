## The champion of the common man

Ramon Magsaysay was unlike the presidents before him. Born in Iba, Zambales, in 1907, he was a mechanic and bus company manager before the war, then a guerrilla leader against the Japanese. As defense secretary he led the campaign that defeated the Huk rebellion, and in 1953 he was elected president in a landslide.[1]

As president he opened Malacañang Palace to ordinary people, traveled constantly to the provinces, and set up a complaints office where farmers and workers could bring their problems directly to the government. His style made him enormously popular. People called him the "Champion of the Masses."[1]

## A trip to Cebu

In March 1957, Magsaysay was nearing the end of his term and preparing to run for re-election that November. On 16 March he flew to Cebu, where he spoke at the commencement of three universities and met with local leaders.[1][4]

In the early hours of 17 March, he boarded the presidential plane for the flight back to Manila. It was a Douglas C-47, a twin-engine military transport, named "Mt. Pinatubo." Aboard were the president, members of his staff and cabinet, military aides, journalists and the crew.[1][3]

## The crash

At about 1:40 a.m., minutes after taking off from Lahug airport, the plane failed to gain enough height over the mountains behind Cebu City. It crashed into the slopes of Mount Manunggal, in the town of Balamban, and burst into flames.[1][3]

Of the people on board, 25 were killed, among them President Magsaysay. Accounts give slightly different totals for the number on board.[2][3]

{{photo:3}}

## The only survivor

One man lived. Nestor Mata, a reporter for the Philippine Herald, was thrown clear of the wreck, badly burned. He later remembered crawling through the dark as the plane burned near him, and calling out for help.[2][4]

Farmers living on the mountain found him the next morning and helped carry him down. He survived his injuries, wrote about the crash for the rest of his life, and died in 2018 at the age of 92.[2] His memoir of the tragedy was published by the University of Santo Tomas.[4]

## The search on the mountain

When the plane did not arrive in Manila, an air and ground search began. Residents of the mountain villages near Balamban had seen the fire and heard the crash in the night, and they led rescuers to the wreckage.[1]

There was nothing left to save. Soldiers and volunteers carried the bodies down the steep trails. The president's body was identified and flown to Manila. Newspapers came out with black-bordered special editions.[1][2]

## What caused it

An investigation concluded that the plane had lost power and could not climb over the mountain, and that the crash was an accident. The aircraft was new to the fleet, with fewer than a hundred hours of flight time.[3]

Because Magsaysay was so popular, and so feared by some, rumors of sabotage spread quickly. No proof of sabotage was ever found.[1]

## A nation in mourning

Radio stations interrupted their programs, churches rang their bells and people wept in the streets. For many poor Filipinos, who had seen him as one of their own, it felt like losing a member of the family.[1]


The news shocked the country. Hundreds of thousands of people lined the streets of Manila for his funeral, one of the largest the city had ever seen. Vice President Carlos P. Garcia was sworn in as president.[1]

Magsaysay was 49 years old. He had been president for just over three years.

## Remembered

The Ramon Magsaysay Award, established in 1957 and often called "Asia's Nobel Prize," honors people across Asia who serve their communities with integrity, in his memory.[1]

On Mount Manunggal, a memorial and a small park mark the crash site. Every 17 March, Cebuanos and officials climb the mountain to remember the president who died there, and the others who died with him.[1]
